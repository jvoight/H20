Here we describe a second construction of elliptic surfaces of maximal rank.  Namely, we
start from an extremal rational elliptic surface and consider a quadratic twist of a 
double cover. 

In order for such a surface to be maximal (assuming rank 0), we must have the following 
properties.  The double cover must be branched at two singular fibres, so that it only
has 6 singular fibres.  Second, the ramified fibres must be unstarred: this means that
they must lie above I_n or II fibres.  Third, the quadratic twist must have exactly 2
additive fibres and these must be starred.  So if the original surface had an unstarred
additive fibre, we must twist above it (there may be ramification there if the type is
II).

Here we present the possibilities in terms of 35 families from Herfurtner (as usual we
ignore one from each of two pairs of semistable isogenous families as well as a surface
that cannot be defined over Q).  The first column in the table is the Herfurtner index.  
There is significant duplication in this table, some explained by obvious isomorphisms,
which we have not attempted to remove.  Also we do not expect to find forms in cases 
where the squarefree part of the discriminant (column "disc") is not square.

Note that twists of the same form can look quite different, e.g. 4, 2 and 4, 4.  I should
probably have tried harder to work out the correct twists.

Some of these ((1,2), (2,1), (5,2)) have all L-polynomials reducible.  Shouldn't that

I do not understand.  For all primes up to 300 we have b_p = r a_p, where a_p, b_p
are the respective Hecke eigenvalues and r is in +-{1/4,1/2,1,2,4}, except for p = 3
for which b_p = 0 (and a_p = 6).  But the LMFDB says that both are twist-minimal.
