In this file we summarize 35 possibilities for elliptic surfaces over P^1
with h^{2,0} = 2.  The middle row of the Hodge diamond is 2, 30, 2, so we
start by looking for examples where the reducible fibres contribute 28 to the
Picard group to go along with the classes of fibre and zero section.

In this context, having an additional section is a codimension-2 condition,
rather than codimension-1 as for K3 surfaces or automatic as for rational
elliptic surfaces, and I don't know any examples where we have rank 27 from
reducible fibres and rank 1.  That doesn't mean there aren't any, of course.

(In contrast to a K3 surface, the genus-1 fibration on a surface of Kodaira
dimension 1 is unique, so there are no lattice games to play.)

The Euler characteristic of an I_n^* fibre is n+2 and it has n components.
Likewise an II^*, III^*, IV^* fibre has Euler characteristic 2 more than
its number of components.  So any fibration with 4 starred fibres should
have maximal Picard number, and we can find them by twisting the 38 fibrations
from Herfurtner's paper "Elliptic surfaces with four singular fibres".
However, it's not necessary to consider both of the isogenous pairs
I9I1I1I1, I3I3I3I3 and I4I4I2I2, I8I2I1I1.  We also omit the III II I6 I1
family because it can only be defined over Q(sqrt -3).

These 35 can be broken up as follows:

(1) In cases 2, 7, 30, 35, the L-polynomials all factor.  In each case we get two 
rational forms of weight 2.

(2) In cases 1, 3, 4, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 23, 24, 25, 28, 29, 32, some L-polynomials are 
irreducible, but the Galois group is always C_2 x C_2.  These subdivide into three types:

(2a) In 6 of these 14, there is a quadratic field contained in all the biquadratic extensions.  It is Q(i) for cases 1, 4, 25, 28; Q(sqrt -2) for 3; and Q(sqrt -6) for 16.

(2b) In 7 of them, namely 13, 14, 15, 18, 23, 24, 29, there is a pair of quadratic fields
of which at least one is contained in all the biquadratics.  I don't have modular forms for 18 or 29.

(2c) That leaves 32, which is somehow connected with the field Q(zeta_12, sqrt(zeta_6-2)).

(3) In the remaining 17 cases 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 19, 20, 21, 22, 26, 27, 31, 33, 34, there is at least one polynomial with Galois group D_4.  These examples remain rather mysterious, being expected to be explained in terms of Hilbert modular forms of odd weight.
