Let S be a singular K3 surface and let pi: S -> P^1 be an elliptic fibration.  If the rank of pi
is 0 and there is a rational set of two multiplicative fibres of pi, we may apply a twist ramified
under that set to obtain a (2,0,2)-surface with an elliptic fibration.  (If the rank is positive,
the twist is very unlikely to have positive rank, so the surface will not be maximal.)  On the
other hand, if there are already two starred fibres, the K3 surface is a twist of a rational 
elliptic surface and we might as well find it that way.  Shimada lists over 700 possible 
reducible fibre configurations for singular K3s, but about half of these do not correspond to
any actual fibration, and another 100 or so have multiple starred fibres.  This leaves about 250
possible configurations, a few of which have two choices for the torsion (and a few more perhaps
admitting different configurations of the same torsion subgroup).  Some of these will be
isogenous to others, but it still seems like a lot to do exhaustively.

Perhaps the natural way to organize the surfaces and their fibrations is by the discriminant of
the K3 surface, which can be any negative integer congruent to 0 or 3 mod 4.

Example.  Consider the K3 surface with a D4E6E8 fibration and thus discriminant -12.  The genus
contains 30 lattices, but only 5 of them have maximal rank:

D4E6E8, trivial torsion
A5D6E7, torsion Z/2Z
3A1 A5D10, torsion (Z/2Z)^2
2A2 A3A11, torsion Z/6Z
D12E6, trivial torsion

All but the third and fourth of these are twists of rational surfaces; for example, the last is a
twist of Herfurtner's I8 II I1 I1 surface.  Thus there are two surfaces that we really need to 
construct.  We recognize the 2A2 A3A11 surface as a double cover of the rational A1A2A3A6 ramified 
at the A1 and A6.  The two A2 fibres and the two I1 fibres are not rational, so we have three 
pairs of rational fibres at which to twist, giving fibre types I12* I4* 2I3 2I1, I12 I4 2I3* 2I1, 
I12 I4 2I3 2I1*.  Only the first of these gives square discriminant and we rapidly find the form
12.3.d.a.

A twist of 3A1 A5D10, to give a rational modular form, would have to be ramified at A5 and one A1.
As before, we can find 3A1 A5D10 as a triple cover of a rational surface with 2A1 D6, totally
ramified at D6 and one A1.  This is the Legendre family $y^2 = x(x-1)(x-t)$, so the twisted 
triple cover is $t(t-1)y^2 = x(x-1)(x-t^3)$.  I don't see a modular form, but this suggests
a new construction: starting from a surface with one starred and two multiplicative fibres, take
a triple cover ramified at the starred fibre and then twist.

(Alternatively one might start by writing down the A5D6E7 starting from the Elkies-Kumar 
construction for discriminant 28, finding the equation

y^2 = x^3 + (3*v^2 - 2*v)*x^2 + (-v^5 + 3*v^4 - 3*v^3 + v^2)*x.)

We now briefly describe the first few discriminants and the corresponding fibrations that have a 
twist relevant for this project.  

*Discriminant -3.*  We start with discriminant -3, where the surface has 6 types of
fibration, but only 4 of maximal rank.  For 3E6 there are no sensible twists, since there are no
unstarred singular fibres.  For 2E8 A2A0 (to avoid hybridizing the notation we use A0 for a
singular irreducible fibre, i.e., I1), the twist is the 2E8D6D4 we have seen elsewhere.  The
remaining examples, A11D7A0A0A0 and A1A1D16A0A0, are isogenous, so we only need to consider one of 
them, say the first one.  We construct it as a triple cover of the A3D5A0 rational family, which is
in fact isogenous to the Legendre surface.  The twist at two nonrational A0 fibres has nonsquare 
discriminant; at A11 and the rational A0, we get 288.3.e.a.

*Discriminant -4.*  A K3 surface of rank 20 and discriminant -4 has three types of fibration that
have maximal rank and at most one starred fibre, namely A9A9A0A0A0A0, A15A3A0A0A0A0, D16A1A1A0A0.
To write down the A9A9, we do a base change of A4A4A0A0 totally ramified at the A4 fibres.
There are two possible twists, but they are isogenous (a 5-isogeny interchanges A4 and A0 fibres),
so it is enough to consider one, and it gives us 128.3.d.c.

For A15A3A0A0A0A0, we can again take a double cover of A7A1A0A0.  There are two rational and two 
conjugate A0 fibres, and so there are 7 possible twists, all having square discriminant.  The
relevant modular forms are 128.3.d.b, 8.3.d.a, with one set of eigenvalues unexplained.

Finally, we can write down A1A1D16 from standard K3 surface methods, finding the equation
y^2 = x^3 + (1/4-3/2*t^2 + t^3)*x^2 + 1/64*x.  All the multiplicative fibres are rational, so
we have 6 twists, all of which give the same coefficients as something on the list of curves
with 4 singular fibres; two each for indices 25, 11, 30.  There is no obvious geometric 
explanation for this.

*Discriminant -7.*  The only suitable fibration is the one with three A6 and three A0 fibres.
(A 7-isogeny exchanges the three rational A6 fibres with the three nonrational A0 fibres.)
If we twist at two A6 fibres (it doesn't matter which), the discriminant is not a square.

*Discriminant -8.*  There are 5 suitable types of fibration, with root sublattices A7A7A3A1,
A11A5A1A1, D14A3A1, A15A1A1A1, E6A11A1 and suitable torsion.

For A7A7A3A1 (note that this too is a double cover of A7A1A0A0), there are 7 rational twists, but 
only 3 with square discriminant.  Two of these give the same unexplained eigenvalues as for 
A15A3, one gives 512.3.f.j.

We can write down A11A5A1A1 as a double cover of the rational surface with A6A3A2A1.  There
is only one twist with vertical rank 28 and square discriminant: namely, we have to twist at
the A11 and A5.  This yields a twist of 512.3.f.a.

A15A1A1A1 is a double cover of A7A1A0A0.  The only twist with square discriminant is at A15 and
the new A1.  It gives the form 512.3.f.j.

K3 surface methods allow us to convert A15A1A1A1 into E6A11A1, but there are no twists with
square discriminant and so nothing to do.  We can also obtain D14A3A1 by K3 surface methods.  Since
the two I1 fibres are conjugate, we can get a square discriminant and a surface defined over Q
only by twisting at the A3A1.  The modular form is 200.3.l.a.

There is nothing for discriminant -11, and for discriminant -12 we have already considered one
of the lattices, so we move on to the other one, the genus of E8E7A2A1.  There are three
lattices that do not already have two starred fibres, with reducible fibres 3A1 3A5,
2A1 A2 D14, and 2A1 D5 A11.

The first of these is a double cover of I1I2I3I6 ramified at I1 and I3.  There is only one twist
with square discriminant, at the two ramified fibres.  We obtain the form 12.3.d.a.

2A1 D5 A11 is a quadratic twist of A1A1D16 from before, at the D16 and an A0 fibre.  The only
quadratic twists with square discriminant are at A11, A0, so these have already been considered.

Finally, starting from 2A1D5A11 we use K3 surface methods to find the fibration with 2A1A2D14.
The only twist giving square discriminant is at the I3 and I1 fibres, and this gives the rather
surprising modular form 1800.3.v.b.
