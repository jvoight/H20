In this file we describe experiments with triple covers.  To start, we consider an extremal 
semistable rational elliptic surface: it has four singular fibres of type $I_{n_j}$ for
$1 \le j \le 4$.  A triple cover totally ramified at two of these will have eight singular
fibres: the ramified fibres will have subscripts multiplied by 3, the unramified ones will 
appear 3 times, and so the subscripts will add to 36, giving a rank of 28 and a surface of
maximal Picard number.  Indeed we always get such a surface by taking a triple cover ramified
only at the singular fibres, although there are four triple covers with partial ramification
at 4 given points and no reason for them automatically to be rational (they correspond to
the subgroups of order 3 in the Jacobian of the genus-1 curve obtained as a double cover
of P^1 branched at the ramification points).

We can also obtain examples from elliptic surfaces with one starred fibre and two $I_n$
fibres from the ramified triple cover.  Also, as in the case of double covers we can allow
partial ramification at an II fibre.  However, total ramification at any additive fibre 
prevents the surface from obtaining maximal rank from its reducible fibres.  But, as we shall 
see, the situation is not hopeless.

I realize that I was too pessimistic about positive rank.  To illustrate the situation,
let us consider the surface from Herfurtner's tables with singular fibre types
$I_1, I_7, II, II$.  This surface has rank 2; in Herfurtner's model the Mordell-Weil
generators are defined over Q(sqrt 3), Q(sqrt -21), though one could twist to make either
of them rational.  Consider a triple cover totally ramified at the two II fibres.  It has 
three I_7 fibres and two I_0^*, which mean you start with Picard number 28, but the sections
pull back to sections and so you get 2 more.  Indeed, this example appears to give the
modular form 21.3.f.c (we had it already, but still).  Note that this idea doesn't apply to 
double covers, or to twists of K3s, because the twist destroys the rank.

For the purely ramified triple covers, note that the condition of the discriminant being a square
is automatic when we have maximal rank.  In the semistable case, this is automatic, because 
we replace two of the $I_n$ fibres by $I_{3n}$, multiplying the discriminant by $3$, and two
by three of the same $I_n$, not affecting it mod squares.  The only other case is that in which
an $II$ fibre ramifies.  In that case we change $II$ to $I_0^*$, which only changes the 
discriminant by a factor of 4 (a square), but the rank is equal to the number of such fibres,
and the triple cover multiplies all height pairings by $3$, so we multiply by $3$ for each such
fibre anyway, just as if it were multiplicative.
